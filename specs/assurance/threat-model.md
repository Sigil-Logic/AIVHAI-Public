---

This is the threat model AIVHAI's assurance case ([#226](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/226)) is stated against.  It says who attacks what, through which interface, and what the project does about each threat.  Every threat in §6 ends in one or more of four places, and the disposition column says which: a **goal** in the assurance case that is to counter it, an **assumption** carried in the case's register, an **exclusion** from the case's scope with its reason (§7), or a **known gap** with an owner (§8).  A threat that names none of the four is a defect in this document.  It is released to the red team with the Sprint 1 hand-off, and [`REDTEAM.md`](../../REDTEAM.md) places it among the other artifacts of the release.

**This is a first draft, and it has not yet been vetted in depth.**  It was written in week five of the project, largely generated automatically by Sigil Logic's HOARDE agents from the published threat models of the components (§9), and it has had only light review.  The AIVHAI team has not yet vetted it in depth.  Feedback from the red team is very welcome, read with that in mind: an error here is as likely to be ours as the system's.

**Its form is open too.**  One possibility we are considering is to organise it with the threat-modelling framework Free & Fair used for VoteSecure.  There, the threat model is codified so that it can be checked mechanically, and it is published both as a static, hyperlinked document and as a dynamic, self-contained interactive application ([VoteSecure v1.4](https://github.com/FreeAndFair/VoteSecure/releases/tag/v1_4); see [`docs/red-team/prior-engagements.md`](../../docs/red-team/prior-engagements.md)).  We would value the red team's view, after looking at that model, of whether such a codification and such a static and dynamic presentation would be useful to them.

The model is built from the explicit threat models already published for the components AIVHAI reasons about, not invented beside them.  §9 records, for each of those sources, what this model adopts, where it diverges, and why, so that a reader of the source can check that nothing was dropped silently.  Every row of §6 cites its source by bibliography key.

The claims this model supports are scoped by [#1](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/1) and [SL-AIVHAI-P04](../../docs/red-team/methodology.md#what-we-claim-and-what-we-do-not): **structural non-bypass, not semantic benignity.**  Nothing here claims that a model or agent is benign, that side channels are defeated, or that availability is provided.

## 1. The system

AIVHAI is a 64-bit CVA6-CHERI application core with the Coral vector and matrix unit integrated **into the core**, running a CHERI compartmentalisation firmware (CF) that isolates mutually distrusting AI models and agents from one another and from a general-purpose OS.  The accelerator shares the core's instruction decode and memory subsystem, and is designed so that its memory accesses are subject to the same MMU, PMP and CHERI checks as every other access by the core ([Robert Watson, 2026-10-09](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/53#issuecomment-6076464211)).  That every vector and matrix access actually passes through those checks is not yet shown: it is threat TM-004 and goal G-c4 of the case, undeveloped.  There is no NPU DMA path.  DMA exists for other peripherals, such as networking, behind the IOPMP and the IOMMU.

The trust boundary is the project's (stated for outside readers in [`methodology.md`](../../docs/red-team/methodology.md#what-we-claim-and-what-we-do-not)): a hardware TCB to be verified (the core, including the vector and matrix datapath, the memory subsystem and tag storage) and a software TCB to be verified (the CF) beneath unverified tenants.  Nothing in this document reports a completed proof; every `G-` disposition is an obligation on the assurance case, not a result.  Tenants are the general-purpose OS, the AI models, and the agents that drive them.

### 1.1 Compartment classes

The CHERIoT Programmers' Guide distinguishes three trust models for a compartment: a **sandbox** isolates untrusted code and trusts its inputs; a **safebox** holds secrets, trusts no input, and is trusted by its callers; **mutual distrust** has both sides defend [@Chisnall-26, §6.4].  AIVHAI's compartments classify as follows, and the classification decides which threats apply to each.

| Compartment                        | Class                                  | Why                                                                                                                                                                                              |
|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tenant model or agent              | Mutual distrust                        | Each tenant is a black box that may be malicious or arbitrarily buggy; no tenant trusts another, and the CF trusts none of them [@Almatary-26b, Security goal; @AmarAndersonChenEtAl-26, §1.2.6] |
| General-purpose OS                 | Mutual distrust, with S-mode privilege | A tenant with architectural privilege over other tenants' address translation but none over the CF.  Treated as compromised, not merely buggy (§9, Smepmp)                                       |
| Compartmentalisation firmware (CF) | Safebox                                | Holds the authority roots and the compartment graph; trusts no input; every tenant's isolation depends on it                                                                                     |
| Peripheral behind IOPMP/IOMMU      | Sandbox, from the core's view          | A bus requester whose transactions are constrained by the IOPMP and IOMMU tables the CF owns; its own firmware is untrusted [@MarkettosBaldwinBukinEtAl-20, §3]                                  |

### 1.2 The two firmware targets

Capabilities Limited has two firmware artifacts, and they are kept distinct here at its author's request [@Almatary-26a; @Almatary-26b]:

- **The Sprint 1 proof-of-concept demo** (`Capabilities-Limited/aivhai-fw-compartment`, `fw_compartment.S`, read here at `9691cf2e0c092f880f0aa57c7c3e70abf95b1b1e` of 2026-10-10): a bare-metal M-mode image that bounds a PCC to a 1 MiB-aligned compartment, strips ASR, clears DDC and all integer registers and eight listed CSRs, and enters the compartment with `jalr`.  It has one compartment, no I/O buffers, no S/U mode, no MMU or PMP, no VME, no IOPMP.  It is Capabilities Limited's Sprint 1 red-team target, and its four deliberately weakenable protections are threats TM-013 to TM-016.
  - **What is attacked.**  The red team supplies the compartment's code as a raw binary, embedded at `compartment_enter` (the build's `FW_COMPARTMENT_PATH`), and attacks only from inside it, after entry.  A successful attack reads, modifies, or obtains capability authority over an asset outside the compartment: the variables `read_var` and `write_var`, which the source marks as assets to reach, and `cheri_infinite_cap`, an unbounded capability the bootstrap leaves in memory outside the compartment [@Almatary-26a, §6].  The bootstrap and the trap and return handlers are outside the attack scope as code to modify, though an attack may exercise the trap and return paths (TM-022).  The objective is spatial: temporal memory safety, timing and side channels, and other architectural properties are outside the demo's attack scope [@Almatary-26a, §6], and availability is out of scope (EXCL-4).
  - **What the demo assumes**, stated by its author on 2026-10-10 in its source and in its instructions [@Almatary-26a, §6].  (i) Every permission other than ASR is left on the compartment's PCC, so that confinement to the compartment's bounds rests on the architecture enforcing tags, bounds and permission monotonicity: G-a1 and assumption A-1.  (ii) Floating-point, vector and counter state is not cleared at entry, because the demo places nothing sensitive there and neither switches context nor takes interrupts; for the CF that state is TM-031 to TM-033.  (iii) Side channels and microarchitectural state are outside its threat model, as they are outside this one (EXCL-2).  (iv) The compartment is padded to 1 MiB alignment so that its bounds are representable, and exact representability still depends on its base and size; whether the bounds a request rounds to stay within the source is TM-002, RP2's crux.
  - **A proof over it is under way, outside this project's case.**  Ben Laurie (Capabilities Limited), working with Claude, is developing a Rocq proof of isolation properties of the prologue over the RVY Sail model.  On 2026-10-11 it found two divergences of the model from the RVY specification, TM-003's sentry `JALR` case and TM-049, which Peter Rugg and Jessica Clarke fixed that day [@Watson-26]; the hand-off's model pin moved to carry the fixes.  The demo's build takes its Sail model from the branch the fixes are on, `0.9.9-undo-branch-restriction`, so on 2026-10-11 the demo and the audited model, `sail-cheri-riscv@84515a8`, are one model; the build follows the branch rather than a commit, so they stay one only while the branch does not move.  The proof is not yet published, and the case does not count it as evidence for G-CF4.
  - **What a capture means.**  With no weakening enabled, reaching an external asset falsifies either the trampoline's boundary invariants (G-CF4) or the ISA's confinement as the Sail model implements it (G-a1, A-1); with a weakening enabled, it is the expected demonstration that the protection is load-bearing.  It is, for Sprint 1, the injection path FETT gave its red team ([#53](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/53) D4): a compartment the red team populates with hostile code, and named assets to reach.
- **The compartmentalisation model draft**: the design the CF will follow — static compartments, bootstrap-closed authority, data-only I/O buffers, synchronous invocation.  Its thirteen core rules are the invariants the G-CF goal of the assurance case will be stated over.

## 6. Threats

Each row is one threat.  The disposition names the assurance-case goal, assumption, exclusion, or gap that answers it.  A goal disposition names the most specific goal of the case ([`aivhai-case.md`](aivhai-case.md), [#226](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/226)) that is to counter the threat; a layer goal, G-a (ISA), G-c (microarchitecture), G-CF (firmware) or G-comp (composition), is named only where no sub-goal is specific to it.  §10 says how the two documents are checked against each other.

## 10. Validation of this document

The documentation gates check this document's form and its bibliography keys.  `scripts/assurance/check-threat-model.py` checks it against the assurance case, in both directions, and runs in `make ci`:

- every `TM-` identifier is unique, and every row names an adversary and an asset defined in §3 and §4, or a dash where the threat has none, a source, and a disposition (the surface is the section's);
- every `G-` disposition is a goal of [`aivhai-case.json`](aivhai-case.json), every `A-` an assumption of its register, every `GAP-` a known gap of §8 that the case carries as a context node, and every `EXCL-` an exclusion of §7 that the case's scope excludes;
- every threat a goal of the case cites names that goal, or one of its sub-goals, in its disposition, so that the case does not claim to counter a threat the threat model sends elsewhere;
- every assumption and gap is used by at least one threat, and every assumption and gap the case carries is defined here.  An exclusion may be a scope statement with no threat of its own, as EXCL-1 is.

A mechanical check in the manner of VoteSecure's `test-validity-of-threat-model.yml` ([#53](https://github.com/Sigil-Logic/AIVHAI-Sigil/issues/53) C1b) is what this approximates; the second, interactive form of the document that VoteSecure also publishes is not yet made.
